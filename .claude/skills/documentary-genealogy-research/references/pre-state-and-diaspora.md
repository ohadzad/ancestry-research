# Before the state and outside Europe — Ottoman, Mandate, consular, Sephardi and Yemenite records

The method was first proven on a Carpathian Shoah case, then run unchanged on a very different
family: Sephardi (Salonika–Jerusalem), Gibraltarian British subjects in Ottoman Jaffa and Gaza,
and Yemenite Jews from Sana'a, meeting at Mikveh Israel in the 1890s and running to 2017. The
prime directives held. The **source stack** was almost entirely different, and so were the
name traps. This file is that stack.

Read it when the family — or one branch of it — was in Ottoman or Mandate Palestine, was under
a foreign consulate's protection, came from Gibraltar / the Maghreb / Salonika / Egypt, or
came from Yemen.

---

## 1. The family is the first archive

The five largest breakthroughs in the source project came from material the family already
held: a MyHeritage tree, a grandfather's autobiographical letter, a printed family book with
primary documents photographed inside it, a published photo collection, and finally the
**estate itself** — editorial correspondence, drafts, annotated personal copies, certificates,
identified photographs. A whole scanning-request plan with a copyright statement was written
for volumes the family turned out to own.

- **Ask what the family holds before building an external route.** At intake, and again
  whenever a target looks like it needs an institution.
- **A printed family book is a secondary family source that embeds primary ones.** Grade the
  embedded scan (`מאומת`), not the book's prose (`כמעט ודאי` at best).
- **Family oral transmission gets its own file** (`docs/family_docs/family-testimony-log.md`):
  who said it, when, what it would close, what would confirm it. Undocumented transmission is
  `טעון אימות` by default — ladder discipline, not doubt about the informant; in the source
  project the tradition was right and the contemporary advertisement looked wrong.
- **A document or research summary handed in by the family or a third party is a lead, not a
  source.** A handed-in summary named three TNA files; the project's own live search returned
  fifteen, and the three strongest were not on the handed-in list. Every one was re-verified
  live before it entered the report.
- **A family-submitted institutional page** (a Palmach veterans profile, a memorial site entry)
  is a family source wearing an institutional URL. Write the grade relativised —
  `מאומת בכרטיס החבר` — so the reader sees what "verified" rests on.

## 2. Consular and pre-state records

Foreign-protected families leave a **consular trail**, and it is the patronymic engine for a
line that predates any civil registry.

| Source | What it yields | Mechanics | Grade it can carry |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Israel State Archives — Registers of British Subjects** (Jaffa, Jerusalem; e.g. `סל-1544/1`, `סל-1545/1`, ~1,000 pp each) | one registration form per person per year, with **Father's Name**, residence, trade, household | volumes have year gaps; an index number is a **register** number, not a form number (an inference the project tested and falsified); the alphabetical index is un-OCR'd — map it by contact sheets at intervals, then render the index page at 300 dpi | content `מאומת`; chaining across volumes `ככל הנראה` |
| **IGRA's mirror of the same registers** (`1838_1908_BritHebNabJer`, `1865_1914_BritishNat_Jaffa`) | the index rows | when IGRA's image URLs 403 for a whole collection, **check the source archive** — the 403 was on IGRA's images only; ISA had the same volume open online for every edition IGRA was logged as "blocked" | index row `ככל הנראה` until the scan is read |
| **TNA consular series FO 78 / FO 841 (Cairo, Beirut) / FO 847 (Alexandria)** | consular-court probate, wills, civil suits naming heirs — often the only death date for a merchant diaspora | nothing digitised; a catalogue description is not the record; **search the given name as if it were a surname** — the clerk filed "Salomon Arwas" under S | existence `מאומת`; identification `טעון אימות` until the file is read |
| **TNA WO 372 medal index cards / WO 329 rolls** | name, corps, rank, number; the roll page puts relatives side by side | Discovery's free viewer yields a watermarked full scan; the **empty** fields disprove family war stories as often as the filled ones confirm them | card `מאומת`; unit identification as stated |
| **Montefiore censuses 1839–1875** | head, spouse, children, ages, **birthplace and year of arrival** (IGRA's index omits the birthplace) | scans carry patronymics the index omits; Hebrew-only spellings (`ארואץ`); the site's search is `POST` and wants a browser UA + Referer, while the PDFs reject a spoofed UA — what looked like a 403 wall was neither | record `מאומת`; lineage chaining `ככל הנראה` |
| **Ottoman nüfus registers 1886–1918** | household registers | digitised for Palestine only; the Yemen vilayet's, if extant, are in BOA Istanbul — a specialist's task, not a database task | — |
| **ISA 1948 Jerusalem census (Mishmar Ha'am)** | 38 fields per person: father's name, birthplace, community, year of immigration, **nationality** | `isa_search` indexes the full resident roster of each file | `מאומת` |
| **Gibraltar National Archives** (1784 list, 1786 orders, 1817 register, censuses to 1914) | surname-indexed residents | a true zero **reframes the question**: `Country` in a consular register is *nationality*, not residence — British Gibraltarian status was held without ever living there | absence `מאומת`; the reading `ככל הנראה` |
| **JewishGen Gibraltar / Sephardi sets** (`JMCGIBRCEN1791`, `GBIRTHS`) | births, census | search by **surname prefix** (3+ letters); run control queries on a known family to prove the engine live before logging a negative | — |

**Field semantics are not stable inside one source.** IGRA's `Country of Origin` read
"Gibraltar" for one man in 1912 and "Palestine" in his other ten registration years — the
column carried nationality in some rows and birthplace in others. Distrust the header; read the
form.

**Catalogue descriptions aggregate across containers.** An ISA description listing ~90 souls
covered the whole container; the target form sat in a sibling file.

## 3. Mandate and Israeli records for a pre-state family

- **IGRA voter rolls** (1944 תש"ד, 1949 Constituent Assembly, municipal rolls to the 1990s) carry
  the **father's name** — the single highest-yield Israeli set. One 1949 line settled three
  questions at once.
- **IGRA post-1948 name changes** (ילקוט הפרסומים) turn a family's self-reported change into a
  documented pattern: 184 `Ṣāleḥ` records established Ṣāleḥ→Zadok as common practice. A name
  change wants **one record printing both forms** plus the population-level pattern.
- **IGRA filter facets are a search surface.** `location: gaza (14)` was a row nobody had
  searched.
- **Mandate marriage/divorce certificates 1921–48**, **1914–17 deportation lists** (USS
  *Tennessee*, Alexandria exiles), **agricultural-workers' conference lists** — all indexed.
- **Burial: JOWBR, BillionGraves, the Chevra Kadisha.** The cheapest route to a death date to the
  day and the father's name carved on the stone; ten family burial records were located this way,
  several fixing a death date to the day. Coverage is
  per-cemetery and a whole cemetery may be in none of them — then a phone call beats every
  index. BillionGraves' free images are 180×135; full resolution is paywalled.
- **JPress: search each Hebrew name plene and defective** — `אהרן` and `אהרון` returned two
  different families. An OCR transcript is a lower evidentiary tier than the downloaded page
  image; save the whole issue, not only the crop (a transcript alone gave the wrong publication
  year once; the neighbouring issues reversed it). Rapid request bursts trip Cloudflare — work
  item by item, with delays.
- **NLI / Israel Archive Network photographs.** A kibbutz archive held eleven family
  photographs; `iiif.nli.org.il` serves them at storage resolution without any impersonation.
  Photographs are graded in **three layers** — cluster metadata, visual content, and *who is
  depicted*; the last is claimed only when the catalogue names them. Exclude a same-name
  performer's material type before sweeping (140 of 141 hits were a singer).
- **Hebrew and Gregorian dates both**, with an independent computational check (`pyluach`):
  "ז' תמוז תרע"ו = 8.7.1916, and it fell on a Sabbath, as the stone says".

## 4. Names in the Sephardi and Arabic-speaking world

The Carpathian rules in `SKILL.md §2` (variant matrix, index orthography, both sides, one
place-identity) apply verbatim. Add:

- **Papponymy.** A grandson carries the grandfather's name. It is a lead and it cuts both ways:
  the *expected* name in a branch is background noise, not a discriminator — a thesis of "if
  the wife is named Simha the link closes" collapsed because Simha was the founder's wife and
  therefore the predicted name.
- **One man, six spellings across one register series** (Arwas / Arruas / Arrwas / Arrowas /
  Aruas / Rawas — two of them alternating inside a single volume), and the index itself
  cross-referencing "See Raw…". Hebrew ארואס / ארואץ /
  ארוואס — versus ארוץ, a genuinely different Sephardi surname.
- **One given name, four forms**: Shlomo / Shalom / Solomon; Eliezer / Azar / Azor / Ezer.
- **A surname can exist in two unrelated diasporas** (Pariente: Maghrebi *and* Salonikan).
  Surname match alone is never affiliation evidence — chain on patronymic, nationality,
  birthplace and arrival year together.
- **A famous rabbinic dynasty of the same name is a trap, not a lead.** The connection is a
  hypothesis for a colophon or a ketubah to test; until there is a record, claim nothing.
- Declare one **transliteration convention** per report: canonical form in prose, the source's
  spelling inside quotes, variants named once.

## 5. Yemen — a branch that grows in depth, not in height

Yemenite Jewry had **no civil registration**, under the Imamate or before. What survives was
made and carried by the community, and there is no archive in Yemen to write to. The classic
routes upward are **scribal colophons and ketubot** — Ktiv (NLI manuscripts), the Ben-Zvi
Institute, JTS, the Bodleian, the British Library — and the Ottoman vilayet registers in
Istanbul, if they exist. Write this down at the start of the branch, before an afternoon is
spent looking for a birth register that was never kept, and expect the branch to be documented
sideways: siblings, the community, the trade, the synagogue.

Where the origin kept no records, **the receiving country's absorption registers are the
substitute spine**: the Operation Magic Carpet / Rosh Ha'ayin immigrant-camp registers on IGRA
(98 `Ṣāleḥ` rows for one family name — shallow indexing, age and camp only, worth two scans and
not a campaign), the 1949 voter roll, the name-change gazette. And date the arrival against the
documented **Yemenite aliyah waves** (general background, not from these projects — verify the
wave years for your period), because a landing at Jaffa usually sits inside a wave with its own
ship lists and press coverage; the source project's subject left Yemen in 1914, in the wake of
Yavnieli's 1911–12 mission.

## 6. Ethics that were tested here

- **Never bypass a block.** A `robots.txt` exclusion, a Cloudflare challenge, a reCAPTCHA
  download, a rate limit: each is a hand-off to the user's own browser, recorded as a blocker.
  The legitimate routes used: the user's browser, and a public IIIF endpoint with no
  impersonation. The house rule was written into the changelog six times because it kept being
  the right call.
- **A whole tree was never published** because it held living relatives' contact details; a
  redacted summary went to the site and the full copy stayed offline. Living-person data is
  minimised by default, whatever the family's own enthusiasm.
- **Draft letters to archives are versioned deliverables** (`docs/letters/`, gitignored) — a
  blocker with a prepared ask is half-cleared.
